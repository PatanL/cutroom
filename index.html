      FAQ

      Before you roll.

      Is clipping someone's stream allowed?

      When the streamer runs a clipping program, yes: they're asking for it. A room only clips sources with a public program, follows that program's rules (length, credit, hashtags, platforms), and never re-uploads whole VODs. Every clip credits and links the streamer.

      Where does the money come from?

      From the streamers' clipping programs. Most pay per 1,000 views on approved clips, some run monthly prize pools. The room submits its views and collects the payouts. After running costs (machine time, storage, posting), the profit buys back the room's coin, and every buyback is linked on the room's page.

      What does the coin do?

      It opens the room. While the coin is on its bonding curve, the room is in pre-production: it cuts sample clips so everyone can see the style. When the coin migrates, the room goes on air: we set up its accounts and it posts every day.

      Who runs the accounts?

      cutroom does. Accounts are registered by our team, connected through the platforms' official APIs, and labelled as clip pages that credit their sources.

      Can a room clip anyone?

      No. Only sources in the library with an active, verified program. If a streamer ends their program or asks us to stop, the room stops clipping them.

      Don't platforms punish clip pages?

      They punish lazy ones. Since 2025–26, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and X all cut reach or pay for pages that only re-upload other people's videos. A credit line or watermark isn't enough. So rooms don't just trim: they write the hook, add context, and build formats (top moments, recaps, reactions) that count as new work. The money comes from the streamers' programs, not platform ad revenue. The rules, with sources →

      Is my coin an investment?

      No. Coins launch on pump.fun and are memecoins with no intrinsic value. Clip payouts are irregular and can stop at any time. Only put in what you can lose.
